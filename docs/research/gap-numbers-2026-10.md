## 1. Three concepts a reader needs first

### (a) Baseline / projected gap (a forecast made before any decisions)
- **Plain English:** "If the city kept doing exactly what it does today, with no new programs, how far short would next year's ongoing money fall of next year's ongoing costs?" It is a forecast made *before* the budget is written. The budget then has to close it.
- **Source definition:** The Outlook "baseline" is made up of ongoing revenues and expenditures; ongoing baseline expenditures are those that maintain, not expand, current service levels. The resulting "Baseline Revenue Shortfall" is the structural budget deficit (IBA 25-36, p.5). OUT27 Table 1.1 labels the same row "(AMOUNT TO BE MITIGATED) / AVAILABLE RESOURCES" (OUT27 p.8).
- **Counting:** One-time resources and expenditures in the prior adopted budget are removed. **No** General Fund or Risk Management reserve contributions are included. No Infrastructure Fund (Prop H) contributions are included. Wages are assumed to rise 2.94%/yr (OUT27 p.7).

### (b) Structural deficit (measured inside a balanced budget)
- **Plain English:** The budget is balanced on paper, but part of it is paid for with money that won't come back next year: savings left over from earlier years, fund balances, or a skipped deposit into the rainy-day reserve. The structural deficit is how much ongoing spending rests on that one-time money.
- **Source definitions:**
  - "Structural budget deficits occur when ongoing and sustainable revenue sources are insufficient to support ongoing expenses." (IBA 25-36, p.4)
  - "Structural budget deficits are persistent, long-term imbalances where government spending regularly exceeds revenue. Key drivers ... are ... when ongoing revenues and resources are insufficient to cover ongoing expenditures, and expenditures that regularly grow at a faster rate than revenues." (IBA 26-11, p.77)
  - 26-15 gives a short form: "structural budget deficit (ongoing services being funded by one-time resources)" (IBA 26-15, p.43).
  - In the IBA table, one-time resources minus one-time expenditures = structural deficit (IBA 26-11, p.78).
- **The key counting choice is the skipped reserve deposit.** IBA: "the suspension of reserve contributions is equivalent to drawing on reserves, which can be considered a one-time resource." The FY2025 and FY2026 adopted budgets counted it as one-time money. The FY2027 Proposed Budget, like the FY2022 and FY2023 budgets, did not (IBA 26-11, p.78). IBA recommends "a consistent approach towards including delayed reserve contributions as a one-time resource" (p.78).

### (c) What "balanced budget" legally means
- Charter Section 69: "The budget as proposed by the Mayor and as adopted by the Council shall be balanced such that proposed expenditures shall not exceed projected revenues **and any other sources to balance the budget**." (CHARTER, Art. VII §69, printed p. VII.1. The same text appears in AB27-FP, p.92.)
- **Plain English:** "Balanced" only means planned spending is no larger than expected money plus any other money the city chooses to use. That other money can include one-time sources. A legally balanced budget can therefore still carry a structural deficit. IBA: "while the City's annual budgets over the past several years were balanced, they were not structurally balanced and relied heavily on one-time resources" (IBA 25-36, p.3). Also: "While the City adopts annual budgets that are balanced each year, as it is legally required to do..." (IBA 26-11, p.77).

## 2. Each figure: what it measures, who produced it, when, and the source row

### $88.8M: FY2027 baseline shortfall (forecast, before any FY2027 decisions)
- **Who and when:** Mayor / Dept. of Finance, Five-Year Outlook released Nov 7, 2025. Reviewed in IBA 25-36 (Dec 8, 2025).
- **Source row:** OUT27 p.8, Table 1.1, row "(AMOUNT TO BE MITIGATED) / AVAILABLE RESOURCES": ($88.8) FY27, ($106.9) FY28, ($52.4) FY29, ($55.3) FY30, ($57.0) FY31. The same row appears as "Baseline Revenue Shortfall" in IBA 25-36 p.5, table "Revised Outlook Shortfall": FY2026 $(37.3), then $(88.8), $(106.9), $(52.4), $(55.3), $(57.0). **Verified, all five match the caller's list.**
- **What it counts:** FY2026 service levels with no new initiatives. Removes FY2026 one-time items. **Excludes** reserve contributions ("Unlike in last year's Outlook, contributions to the General Fund and Risk Management reserves are not included in baseline shortfall amounts", IBA 25-36 p.5). Excludes "Additional Priorities Beyond Baseline".
- **Related figure: $110.6M.** This adds the Outlook's "Additional Priorities Beyond Baseline" (homelessness funding and new or expanded facilities): $(21.8) FY27 → "Revised Outlook Shortfall" $(110.6) FY27, $(139.0) FY28, $(91.5) FY29, $(95.0) FY30, $(104.4) FY31 (IBA 25-36 p.5; text p.6).
- IBA also calls the $88.8M "The structural budget deficit projection for the FY 2027 Outlook ... (at FY 2026 service levels)" (IBA 25-36 p.7). So "baseline deficit" and "structural deficit" are used interchangeably at the forecast stage. This is a source of confusion.

### $118.0M: updated FY2027 baseline deficit (April 2026, still before budget decisions)
- **Who and when:** Reported by IBA 26-11 (Apr 29, 2026); the Mayor reports the same figure in MAYOR-MSG.
- **Source text:** "In November 2025 the Mayor released the FY 2027-2031 Five-Year Financial Outlook, which projected a baseline shortfall of $88.8 million in FY 2027. Since then, increases to the City's pension payment and decreases in revenue growth have increased that baseline deficit to $118.0 million." (IBA 26-11 p.3). Also p.5: "This shortfall further grew to $118.0 million due to current-year revenue declines and increased mandatory FY 2027 expenditures." MAYOR-MSG p.1 (unnumbered): "that shortfall worsened to approximately $118.0 million due to lower transient occupancy tax revenue, increased pension costs, and weaker departmental revenue."
- **What it counts:** Same baseline concept as $88.8M. It excludes reserves: "unlike prior years, that deficit assumes no contributions to City reserves ... if contributions to the General Fund ($80.1 million) and Risk Management Reserves ($29.6 million) had been included, the deficit would have increased to $227.7 million." (IBA 26-11 p.3, footnote 3). $29.2M of it is assumed wage increases (p.3, footnote 2).
- **Path from $118.0M to a balanced budget (IBA 26-11 pp.5-6):** $118.0M + $28.2M in Proposed Budget expenditure additions = **$146.2M** "shortfall that needs to be solved for". Minus $26.2M in assumed labor-negotiation savings leaves **$120.0M** "to be filled through revenue increases and expenditure reductions". Major GF mitigation reductions total $66.4M and 236.76 FTE (p.4).

### $11.2M: structural deficit remaining in the FY2027 Proposed Budget (IBA, excluding the skipped reserve deposit)
- **Who and when:** IBA 26-11, Apr 29, 2026.
- **Source row:** IBA 26-11 p.78, table "General Fund Structural Deficit Over Time ($ in millions)", FY2027 column: One-Time Resources $24.8; One-Time Expenditures 13.6; Structural Deficit (11.2); Suspended General Fund Reserve Contributions 80.1; Revised Structural Deficit (including reserves) (91.3); Revised Structural Deficit (excluding reserves) (11.2). Also in the text on p.10: "$24.8 million in one-time resources ... only $13.6 million in one-time expenditures ... the remainder ... ($11.2 million) going to support ongoing expenses." **Verified.**
- **What it counts:** This is *after* the Proposed Budget's decisions. It excludes the $80.1M skipped GF reserve deposit. Including that deposit gives **$91.3M**. Neither version includes the skipped Risk Management reserve contributions (p.78, footnote 1).
- **IBA caveat:** "several items that were considered one-time resources in the past, such as the suspension of reserve contributions, are no longer being counted towards the structural imbalance stated in the budget, and consequently the structural imbalance may be understated." (p.10)

### $15.3M / $95.4M: structural deficit after the May Revision (IBA)
- **Who and when:** IBA 26-15, June 2, 2026.
- **Source text:** "Although the structural budget deficit (ongoing services being funded by one-time resources) in FY 2027 as of the May Revision is $15.3 million, it increases to $95.4 million when including deferred payments to reserves." (IBA 26-15 p.43). Attachment 1, p.1: the May Revise has $8.8M one-time resources and $4.8M one-time expenditures; the extra $4.1M "increases the structural deficit to $15.3 million". Footnote 1 on that page says the $15.3M excludes suspended GF reserve contributions and the figure is $95.4M when they are included.
- KPBS's headline calls $15.3M a "gap" the council "will have to close". That is a different thing (see Contradictions).

### $23.1M: net structural deficit in the FY2027 Adopted Budget (city budget office)
- **Who and when:** Mayor / Dept. of Finance, FY2027 Adopted Budget Vol. 1 Executive Summary (posted Aug 2026).
- **Source text (AB27-ES p.5, section "Financial Outlook and Stability"):** "One-time resources of $40.8 million support $17.7 million in one-time uses for a net structural deficit of about $23.1 million, or about 1.0 percent of General Fund expenditures; an improvement over Fiscal Year 2026." The same paragraph: the budget "continues to delay the planned General Fund reserve contribution of approximately $80.1 million ... however, the Budget does include a modest contribution to the General Fund Reserve up to $1.7 million, pending availability of excess equity at year-end", and "also delays risk management reserve contributions of approximately $36.7 million." **Verified, p.5 (printed "-5-").**
- **What it counts:** After all adopted-budget decisions. It **does not** count the $80.1M skipped GF reserve deposit as one-time money. It does not count the $36.7M delayed Risk Management contributions.
- **$103.2M "IBA-convention" version: not found in any published document.** 23.1 + 80.1 = 103.2 **[arithmetic, not a published figure]**. I searched every downloaded document for "103.2" and got no match. The IBA has published the reserve-inclusive version for the Proposed ($91.3M) and May Revise ($95.4M) stages, but not for the Adopted Budget. Both of those IBA pairs differ by exactly $80.1M, so the arithmetic follows the IBA method. Still, the explainer should say "if you also count the skipped $80.1M reserve deposit, it would be about $103M (our arithmetic)", or use the IBA's published May Revise pair instead.
- **Later event:** The $1.7M reserve contribution will not happen. IBA 26-25 p.12: "as there is no year-end excess equity, this is no longer expected to occur."

### $203.8M: peak structural deficit, FY2025 Adopted Budget (IBA)
- **Source row:** IBA 26-11 p.78, same table, FY2025 column: One-Time Resources $222.1; One-Time Expenditures 18.3; Structural Deficit (203.8); Suspended GF Reserve Contributions 22.4; Revised Structural Deficit (including reserves) (203.8); Revised Structural Deficit (excluding reserves) (181.4). Text: "That imbalance peaked in FY 2025" (p.78). **Verified.**
- **What it counts:** The FY2025 Adopted Budget, after decisions. It *includes* $22.4M of skipped reserve deposits counted as one-time money, because the FY2025 budget documents counted them that way (p.78, footnote 1). On the consistent excluding-reserves basis, FY2025 is still the peak at **$181.4M**.
- **Full series (IBA 26-11 p.78), Structural Deficit row:** FY22 96.7 | FY23 38.3 | FY24 99.5 | FY25 203.8 | FY26 103.6 | FY27 (Proposed) 11.2.
  Including reserves: 107.4 | 62.6 | 99.5 | 203.8 | 103.6 | 91.3.
  Excluding reserves: 96.7 | 38.3 | 99.5 | 181.4 | 48.0 | 11.2.

### ~$30M: FY2028 gap (IBA, as reported by KPBS)
- **Source:** KPBS, June 9, 2026 (web, no page). Reporter's paraphrase: "That 'structural' budget deficit will grow to around $30 million next year, according to Modica," because of the loss of Balboa Park parking revenue and lower trash fees for single-family homes under a city settlement. ### Other related numbers found (for the reconciliation)
- **$258.2M:** FY2026 shortfall projected in the previous Outlook (Dec 2024). "Last year's Outlook, released December 2024, projected a budget shortfall of $258.2 million for FY 2026." (IBA 25-36 p.3)
- **$318M:** MAYOR-MSG p.1 (unnumbered): "Last year, our city was staring down a $318 million structural budget deficit ... We solved $270 million of that deficit..." **How $318M was built is not explained in any document I found** (see Contradictions).
- **$37.3M:** FY2026 baseline shortfall measured on the FY2026 Adopted Budget with one-time items removed and reserves excluded; it is **$103.6M** if the $66.4M of target reserve contributions are added (IBA 25-36 p.5). The FY2026 *structural deficit* in IBA 26-11 p.78 is also $103.6M, computed differently (115.2 one-time resources − 11.6 one-time expenditures). The two concepts happen to produce the same number.
- **$227.7M:** the $118.0M FY2027 baseline if the GF ($80.1M) and Risk Management ($29.6M) reserve contributions were included (IBA 26-11 p.3, footnote 3).
- **$4.7M:** FY2026 actually closed with a $4.7M draw on the General Fund Stability Reserve, "the first time in recent history". Risk reserves ended $20.8M below Adopted Budget projections (IBA 26-25 pp.1-2, 12, 17). The reserve falls from $207.1M to $202.4M, 10.9% of operating revenues against a 14.1% target (p.12). This is a year-end cash result, not a gap forecast.

## 4. Forward projections (FY2028 and later)

### Latest published numbers
1. **Five-Year Outlook (Nov 2025; this is still the latest official forecast).** Baseline: FY28 $106.9M, FY29 $52.4M, FY30 $55.3M, FY31 $57.0M (OUT27 p.8). With priorities beyond baseline: $139.0M, $91.5M, $95.0M, $104.4M (IBA 25-36 p.5). These were made *before* the FY2027 cuts, so they are stale for FY2028.
2. **IBA, June 2, 2026:** "While mitigations included in the FY 2027 Proposed Budget reduce these budget gaps, a projected deficit of $32.9 million remains in FY 2028 (as estimated at the time of the Proposed Budget), indicating that reductions will be needed again in next year's budget." (IBA 26-15 p.43). Repeated on p.48: "that deficit nevertheless will persist into FY 2028, where a projected gap of $32.9 million will require additional difficult decisions." The figure "exclude[s] required reserve contributions" (p.43). **Not found:** which document first published the $32.9M, and its FY2029+ values.
3. **Costs that came after the $32.9M estimate (IBA 26-15 pp.44-45).** The May 20, 2026 tentative settlement lowers solid waste fees for FY2028-29 and repeals Balboa Park paid parking by the end of calendar 2026.
   - Solid Waste Management Fund rate revenue falls $40.9M in FY2028 and $44.7M in FY2029. After possible mitigations, "the estimated impact to the General Fund is approximately $10.8 million in FY 2028, and $10.0 million in FY 2029" (p.45).
   - Balboa Park parking: "in FY 2028 the net financial impact associated with the elimination of the Balboa Park Paid Parking Program totals $9.4 million" (p.44).
   - IBA does not publish a combined FY2028 total with these items added to the $32.9M. **Combined figure: not found.**
4. **FY2029 outlook is worsening (IBA 26-15 p.43):** "Despite a small surplus that was previously projected to be reached in FY 2029, there are significant cost pressures in the short term that will likely reverse this surplus." Tentative labor agreements with two unions include raises "largely deferred until FY 2029", which "could add significant costs beginning in FY 2029". **Not found:** the source and size of the "small surplus previously projected" for FY2029. The Nov 2025 Outlook showed a $52.4M FY2029 deficit, not a surplus.
5. **IBA 26-25 (Oct 20, 2026, the newest IBA statement):** no FY2028 gap number. Its conclusion: "many of the mitigation measures used in recent years have already been implemented, limiting the options available to address future budget shortfalls. Given the City's structural deficit, reduced reserve levels, and limited budget flexibility, conservative revenue and expenditure projections will be particularly important in future budget development." (p.17). Relevant to FY2027-28: EMS Fund balance is $5.3M below what the FY2027 Adopted Budget assumed, which "will need to be closely monitored ... to determine if the budgeted level of General Fund support will need to be reduced" (p.16).

### Has an updated outlook or a first-quarter report come out?
- **No, not as of 2026-10-26.** The General Fund FY2028-2032 Five-Year Financial Outlook and the FY2027 First Quarter Budget Monitoring Report are scheduled for release on **Friday, November 6, 2026**, with B&GE Committee review on Nov 18 and City Council review on Dec 14, 2026 (IBA 26-20, Key Dates attachment pp.1-2). A web search found no early release. **FY2028-2032 Outlook figures: not found (not yet published).**

### Assumptions that drive the projections (Nov 2025 Outlook)
- **Pension (ADC):** Taken from the SDCERS actuarial valuation as of June 30, 2024 (6.5% investment return/discount rate, 3.25% payroll growth). GF ADC is $383.0M FY27, $394.4M FY28, $340.1M FY29 (OUT27 p.8, p.41). FY2029 drops "approximately $76.5 million ... or $54.2 million for the General Fund, because the City will have fully paid some of the unfunded actuarial liability associated with amortization layers" (OUT27 p.41). That explains why the deficit "drop[s] in half in FY 2029" (IBA 25-36 p.2).
  - Update: the actual FY2027 GF pension payment rose $18.0M to $396.1M, mainly because "salary increases were higher than expected" (IBA 26-11 pp.9-10). The FY2028 ADC depends on FY2026 investment returns (known around Dec 2026/Jan 2027) and an experience study expected to affect the FY2028 ADC (IBA 26-11 p.76).
- **Labor contracts:** 2.94% annual general wage increase FY27-31, equal to the 15-year San Diego CPI average (OUT27 p.7; IBA 25-36 p.16). A 1% GF wage increase costs about $9.9M including overtime, not counting the lagged pension effect (IBA 25-36 p.16). Later, tentative agreements defer raises to FY2029 (IBA 26-15 p.43).
- **Sales tax:** +2.5% FY27, +3.0% FY28, +3.2% in the outer years, in line with the city's sales tax consultant (Oct 2025). IBA says it "does not factor in any potential near-term recession"; UCLA Anderson's 1.8% would mean $2.6M less in FY27 (IBA 25-36 p.10). The adopted FY2027 budget assumes sales tax +2.0% (AB27-ES p.5).
- **Overall revenue:** +1.4% in FY27, then "growth averaging about 3.2% per year", below the 6.2% average inflation of FY2021-25 (IBA 25-36 p.9).
- **Trash fee:** The fee revenue sits in the Solid Waste Management Fund, not the General Fund. It helps the GF by removing trash costs: "because trash collection had previously been funded by the City's General Fund, implementation of the trash fee relieved the General Fund of the obligation to fund most trash collection expenditures." (IBA 26-11 p.3, footnote 4). The Outlook assumed a flat $3.0M GF assistance program, plus one-time FY2026 GF costs ($10.0M for eligible customers, $5.0M for ineligible customers) (IBA 25-36 pp.27-28). The settlement now adds about $10.8M FY28 and $10.0M FY29 to GF costs (IBA 26-15 p.45).

### What is NOT included in the projections
- **Reserve contributions:** no GF or Risk Management reserve contributions in any Outlook year (OUT27 p.7; IBA 25-36 p.5, p.31). Including them would have made FY2027 "significantly higher" (IBA 25-36 p.31): $227.7M instead of $118.0M (IBA 26-11 p.3, fn 3).
- **Infrastructure:** "the Outlook does not include the full funding of capital infrastructure projects that were identified in the City's most recent Five-Year CIP Outlook" (IBA 25-36 p.6, fn 4). The CIP gap was $6.51B (IBA 25-36 p.7) and is now $7.81B over five years (IBA 26-11 p.9).
- **No Prop H Infrastructure Fund contributions** (OUT27 p.7).
- **Library Ordinance (6%) funding and full Penny for the Arts** are not included (IBA 25-36 p.8, p.28).
- **Recession:** "unpredictable events such as an economic recession could significantly alter projections" (IBA 25-36 p.6).
- **Other Council priorities** (IBA 25-36 p.6).

## 5. Why the gap keeps coming back: plain-language IBA sentences (with cites)
- "Overall baseline revenues increase steadily for the remaining four years of the Outlook, with growth averaging about 3.2% per year. Notably, this growth rate is lower than the average annual rate of inflation over the prior five fiscal years (FY 2021-2025) of 6.2%..." (IBA 25-36 p.9)
- "Although baseline revenue projections over the Outlook increase steadily ... they are insufficient to cover projected baseline expenditures." (IBA 25-36 p.5). For FY2027 alone, baseline spending rises 5.4% ($116.9M) (IBA 25-36 p.14) against 1.4% revenue growth (p.9).
- "On a macro level, over recent years the City's expenses to provide services – including costs for personnel, supplies, contracts, and materials – have grown at a pace equal to or in some cases exceeding overall inflation, while the City's major General Fund revenues have grown at a slower pace." (IBA 26-11 p.77)
- "...the City's major General Fund revenues are only keeping pace with inflation, and not growing at a faster rate." (IBA 26-11 p.4)
- "...the City must make substantial progress towards eliminating the structural budget deficit now to avoid repeating a cycle whereby painful programmatic cuts need to be identified year after year." (IBA 26-11 p.3)
- "These conditions reflect an ongoing misalignment between the City's available revenues and the cost of maintaining current service levels." (IBA 26-15 p.48)
- "...a substantial gap remains between the City's ongoing revenues and the ongoing expenses associated with maintaining current service levels." (IBA 25-36 p.37)
- History: "This overall macro-level imbalance dates back decades ... the City's major General Fund revenues, in comparison to other peer cities, are relatively low, while expectations of and demands for City services are not." (IBA 26-11 p.77)

## 6. Proposed presentation for a layperson

**Rule for the page: one concept per question, one source per series, and the counting choice printed next to every number.**

**Q1. "How big is the problem this year (FY2027)?"** **Headline: $118 million.**
- *Why lead with it:* It is the size of the hole the city had to fill to write a legally balanced FY2027 budget. The Mayor (MAYOR-MSG p.1) and the IBA (26-11 p.3) state the same number, and press coverage used it. It is a before-decisions number, which is what "the problem" means to most people.
- *Counting stated:* ongoing costs vs ongoing revenue at current service levels; **does not** include rebuilding reserves.
- *Follow-up line:* "The budget was balanced, as the law requires. About $23 million of ongoing spending still rests on one-time money (city budget office, Adopted Budget p.5). That rises to about $103 million if you also count the $80 million rainy-day deposit the city skipped (our arithmetic; the IBA counts skipped deposits this way, but it has not published an Adopted-Budget figure)." For a published IBA pair, use the May Revision figures: $15.3M / $95.4M (IBA 26-15 p.43).

**Q2. "How big was it at its worst?"** **Headline: $204 million (FY2025 adopted budget).**
- *Source and counting:* IBA's structural-deficit series (IBA 26-11 p.78). It is the ongoing spending that rested on one-time money, *including* $22.4M of skipped reserve deposits, which is how the FY2025 budget itself counted them. On a consistent basis without reserves, FY2025 is still the worst year at $181M.
- *Why this and not $258M or $318M:* Those are a different kind of number (a forecast before fixes, or an unexplained Mayor's figure). Only the IBA table measures every year from FY2022 to FY2027 the same way, so the reader can watch the series fall: 204 → 104 → 11-23.

**Q3. "What's coming?"** **Headline: about $33 million for FY2028, before new settlement costs, and to be replaced on Nov 6, 2026.**
- *Source:* IBA 26-15 p.43 ($32.9M, "as estimated at the time of the Proposed Budget", excludes reserve contributions). Print beside it the separately listed new costs the IBA identified: about $10.8M more GF trash subsidy and a $9.4M parking revenue loss in FY2028 (IBA 26-15 pp.44-45). IBA gives no combined total, so don't add them up in a headline. Note that the IBA expects FY2029 to get worse (deferred raises).
- Label the Nov 2025 Outlook's $106.9M FY2028 figure as "the old forecast, made before this year's cuts".
- Add a dated banner: "The city's new five-year forecast is due Nov 6, 2026" (IBA 26-20).

**Reconciliation graphic (FY2027 waterfall, all published):**
$88.8M (Nov 2025 forecast) → +$21.8M planned additions = $110.6M (Outlook view) | separately, re-forecast → **$118.0M** (Apr 2026) → +$28.2M must-do additions = $146.2M → −$26.2M assumed labor savings = $120.0M closed with revenues and cuts → **balanced budget**, leaving a structural deficit of $11.2M (Proposed) → $15.3M (May Revise) → **$23.1M (Adopted, city count)**. Footnote: add $80.1M for the skipped reserve deposit (IBA convention). The ultimate reserve-inclusive baseline would have been $227.7M.

**Glossary box (three lines):**
- *Projected/baseline gap* = forecast shortfall if nothing changes.
- *Structural deficit* = the part of a balanced budget propped up by one-time money.
- *Balanced* = spending ≤ revenue **plus any other sources** (Charter §69). One-time money is allowed.

## 7. Contradictions, ambiguities and cautions
1. **$203.8M vs $203.7M.** The IBA 26-11 text says "That structural imbalance totaled $203.7 million in the FY 2025 Adopted Budget" (p.3). The same report's table says (203.8) (p.78). Probably rounding; use the table.
2. **The "Structural Deficit" row in IBA 26-11 p.78 is not consistent across years.** FY2025-26 include skipped reserve deposits; FY2027 does not. Compare only within the "including reserves" or "excluding reserves" rows. Comparing $203.8M (FY25, incl.) with $11.2M (FY27, excl.) overstates the improvement. Consistent comparisons: 203.8 → 91.3 (incl.) or 181.4 → 11.2 (excl.).
3. **City vs IBA structural deficit for FY2027.** The city reports $23.1M (Adopted, excl. reserve). The IBA's published figures are $11.2M (Proposed) and $15.3M (May Revise) excl. reserve, and $91.3M and $95.4M incl. reserve. The rise from 15.3 to 23.1 between May Revise and adoption is consistent with council changes, but **no document reconciling the two was found**. $103.2M is **not published anywhere I found**.
4. **Risk Management reserves differ by stage.** IBA used $29.6M (26-11 p.3, fn 3, Proposed). The Adopted Budget says it delays about $36.7M (AB27-ES p.5). None of the reserve-inclusive structural figures ($91.3M, $95.4M) include RM reserves (26-11 p.78, fn 1).
5. **$318M (Mayor) vs $258.2M (Dec 2024 Outlook) for FY2026.** MAYOR-MSG calls $318M "last year's structural budget deficit" and says $270M was "solved". IBA 25-36 p.3 cites the prior Outlook's $258.2M FY2026 shortfall. **How $318M was built: not found.** Avoid it, or quote it as "the Mayor's figure".
6. **Same number, two concepts: $103.6M.** The FY2026 baseline shortfall including $66.4M of reserves (IBA 25-36 p.5) and the FY2026 adopted structural deficit (IBA 26-11 p.78) are both $103.6M, built from different components. Do not present them as one measure.
7. **KPBS labelling (June 9, 2026).** The headline and text call the $15.3M a gap "the council will have to close". The IBA defines it as the structural deficit inside a budget that is already balanced (26-15 p.43). KPBS also says the $118M "could be as high as $146 million when including another $26 million for legal mandates...". IBA 26-11 gives $28.2M of additions (to $146.2M) and, separately, $26.2M of assumed labor savings (p.5-6). The "$26 million" label appears to mix these up. KPBS's "around $30 million" for FY2028 is a paraphrase; the IBA's written figure is $32.9M, and that figure predates the settlement costs KPBS links it to. **Whether Modica's "~$30M" includes the settlement impacts: not determinable from the sources.**
8. **"Small surplus previously projected ... in FY 2029"** (IBA 26-15 p.43) conflicts with the Nov 2025 Outlook's $52.4M FY2029 deficit (OUT27 p.8). The newer projection behind it was **not found**.
9. **IBA 25-36 sales tax typo?** The text says the First Quarter Report projects FY2026 sales tax "to end the year at $347.1 million" (p.10). The table on p.9 shows $374.1M for the FY2026 projection. Probably transposed digits; use the table.
10. **IBA 26-25 internal mismatch:** revenues ended "$22.3 million below budgeted amounts" (p.2, overview) vs "$22.4 million below the Adopted Budget" (p.2, revenue section). Immaterial.
11. **Reserve-shortfall wording:** IBA 25-36 p.32 projected an FY2027 reserve shortfall of about $80.4M if the deposit was skipped. Later documents use $80.1M (26-11 p.3; AB27-ES p.5). These are different estimate dates.
12. **IBA 25-36 calls the $88.8M forecast a "structural budget deficit projection"** (p.7). So the IBA itself sometimes uses "structural deficit" for a forecast baseline gap. The explainer should define its own terms and not rely on the documents' word choice.
